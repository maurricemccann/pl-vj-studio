PL Third Eye VJ Studio

A painting app for your hand. Everything it does, in the order you will use it.

Tap any picture to enlarge it. Pinch or scroll to zoom, drag to move.

1Start here

What Third Eye is

A live instrument for the house ribbons. Five pieces, each a few curves on a ground, and every part of them can be moved while they play: the shape, the width, the gap between them, the colour, the paint they are made of, and the motion. It runs on Windows in its own window, and it hands a controller to your phone over your own Wi-Fi, with no account and nothing sent anywhere.

Good to know Pick a piece, drag a white dot on the canvas, move a slider. Everything else on this page is here for when you want it.
Good to know Nothing is saved. A piece goes back to how it came when you choose it again or open the app again. The app is for playing, not for keeping.

The three ways to play it

The window, the phone, and your hands. All three move the same picture at the same time.

How to

  1. The window has the canvas and every control. It is the only place that draws the ribbons.
  2. The phone opens the address shown under the frame time in the sidebar. It gets the ribbon list, the pads, the ribbon settings, the adjustments and the effects, and no canvas.
  3. Your hands open the same address with /hands.html on the end, on a laptop or a phone with a camera. Pinch, raise, roll and spread, and the ribbon follows.
Good to know The phone and the hands page need the window open. The window is what serves them.

Keys

Keys work when the pointer is not in a text box. The four letter keys need a point selected on the canvas.

FPresentation mode on or off: full screen, no sidebar
EscapeLeave presentation mode
HGive the selected point handles, or take them away
KSwing the other handle into line with the one you moved last
TTie or untie the two handles of the point
WPut the width handle of the point back to normal
Wheel over a selected segmentUp adds a point in the middle of it, down takes away the point after it
Shift and drag a sliderFine adjustment
Double-click a slider numberType an exact value

2The screen

The window

The canvas fills the window. The sidebar sits on the right, in the house cream and gold. A small round button in the top right corner of the canvas turns presentation mode on.

The sidebar, top to bottom: the wordmark, the frame time line, the phone address, the piece menu, the Ribbon list with All ribbons and Just this one under it, the tab rail with its six tabs and the property view beside it with a search box at the top, the two pads, the Curve graph, the frequency meter, and a line of hints.

The canvas is where the ribbons draw, and where you reshape them: the white dots, the blue handles, the orange width handle and the gold and red segment marks all live there.

The Ribbon list and the group

Every ribbon of the piece is a row: a box, a swatch of its ink, and its number. One ribbon is the one you are editing. Any number of them can be in the group.

How to

  1. Tap a row to make that ribbon the one you are editing. Its white dots appear on the canvas and the Ribbon tab shows its numbers. The row gets a gold bar at its left.
  2. Tick the box on a row to add it to the group. The title of the Ribbon tab says how many are selected.
  3. All ribbons puts every ribbon in the group. Just this one leaves only the ribbon you are editing.
  4. A slider in the Ribbon tab moves every ribbon in the group by the same amount, each from where it was. A colour, a material or a blend mode is set the same on all of them.
  5. The pads, the width handle and the dots on the canvas work on the one ribbon you are editing, never the group.
Good to know Clicking a dot or a curve of another ribbon on the canvas selects that ribbon and empties the group down to it.
Good to know The Animation, Modulation and hand controls also work on the group: shuffle, keyframe, the LFOs, sound and light all move every ribbon that is ticked.

The six tabs and the search box

The tab rail stands at the left of the property view. Tap an icon and the view shows that tab’s controls. Ribbon is first and opens first.

How to

  1. Type in the search box and the tab shows only the controls whose name, or whose group’s name, contains what you typed. Clear it to see everything again.
  2. The search is for the tab you are on. Switch tabs and it still applies.
RibbonThe material, numbers and colours of the ribbon you are editing, or of the group
CurveClosed loop, Sketch new ribbon, and the key hints
AnimationShuffle, Ambient wander, Preset A / B morph, Keyframe
ModulationThe microphone, the camera, LFO 1 and LFO 2
AdjustmentsHue, saturation, brightness, colour balance, the tone curve, the gradient map
EffectsOpacity, noise, sharpen, bloom, glitch, halftone, chromatic aberration

Sliders, colours and menus

Every number in the tabs is a slider with its value at the right. Every colour is a swatch and a hex code. Every list is a menu.

How to

  1. Click or drag anywhere on a slider track and the value jumps there, the way a normal slider does.
  2. Hold Shift while you drag and the slider moves finely from where it is.
  3. Double-click the number to type an exact value. Enter keeps it, Escape leaves it as it was.
  4. Roll the wheel over a slider to move one step.
  5. Tap a colour swatch to open the colour picker, or type a hex code such as #c9a84c in the box beside it. The colour changes as soon as the code is complete.
  6. Tap a menu to open it and tap a line to choose. Escape, or a tap outside, closes it.

The two pads

Two squares under the tabs, each with a gold dot. Drag the dot and two numbers of the ribbon change at once.

How to

  1. Width x Taper: left to right is the width, top to bottom is the taper.
  2. Gap x Tremor: left to right is the gap, top to bottom is the tremor.
  3. Press and drag the dot. The ribbon changes as you move. Lift, and it stays.
  4. The pads move the one ribbon you are editing, not the group.
Good to know The dot stays where you last put it. It does not move to show a value you changed with a slider or from the phone.

The frame time line

Under the wordmark, a small line of numbers says how long each frame took to draw. It is a measurement, not a feeling.

render 3.2msThe time to draw the whole frame, ribbons and all
postFX 1.1ms (GL)The time the adjustments and effects took, on the graphics card
CPU fallback: ...The graphics card could not be used, so the effects are worked out pixel by pixel; the reason follows
A warning signThe frame took longer than a sixtieth of a second
material error: ...A material failed to draw; the ribbon shows as the plain fill instead
Good to know The line is empty until an adjustment or an effect is on; the ribbons on their own are cheap.

3Pieces and scenes

The five pieces

Choose a piece from the menu at the top of the sidebar. Each one is a ground and a handful of ribbons, with its own widths, gaps and inks.

How to

  1. Pick a piece. Ribbon 1 becomes the one you are editing and the group is just that one.
  2. Changing the piece forgets presets A and B, stops the keyframe, and clears any point or segment you had selected.
  3. The phone changes with you: a piece chosen on either side opens on both.
The DirectionSix indigo ribbons running off every edge of a white ground
The Direction, DenserThe same six on a dark ground, wider, blue with a green core
Whispers of Gold (VJ test 1)Two gold ribbons, one broad and one fine, on parchment
Two-Scale Accumulation (VJ test 4)Three gold ribbons on near black, a little shake in each
Emissive Glow (VJ test 12)Two cream ribbons that glow, on near black
Good to know The pieces live inside the app. There is no file to open and nothing to save; what you change is for the set you are playing.

Shaping a ribbon on the canvas

A ribbon is a smooth curve through a few points. The points of the ribbon you are editing show as white dots. Move them and the ribbon moves.

How to

  1. Drag a white dot to move that point. The ribbon follows as you drag.
  2. A short press on a dot, without moving it, selects the point. It turns gold.
  3. Move the pointer along the ribbon and the segment under it lights gold. Click it and it turns red: the segment is selected.
  4. With a segment selected, roll the wheel up to add a point in the middle of it, and down to take away the point after it. A ribbon keeps at least three points.
  5. Click a dot or a segment of another ribbon and that ribbon becomes the one you are editing.
  6. Double-click a dot or a segment to select it with no chance of moving it.
Good to know The dots and marks are guides. They are never drawn in presentation mode and never go into a recording.

Handles, tied ends and the width handle

A point can carry two blue handles that bend the curve through it, and an orange handle that sets how thick the ribbon is right there.

How to

  1. Select a point and press H. Two blue squares appear on a line through the point. Drag one and the curve bends through it.
  2. The two handles start tied: move one and the other mirrors it, so the curve stays smooth through the point. Press T to untie them and move each on its own. Press T again to tie them.
  3. Press K to swing the other handle into line with the one you moved last.
  4. Press H again and the handles go. The segment goes back to the plain smooth curve through its points.
  5. The orange dot on a short stem beside a selected point is the width handle. Drag it away from the ribbon to make the ribbon thicker at that point, towards it to make it thinner, from about a sixth of the width to three times it. The thickness eases into the neighbouring points. W puts it back.
Good to know A ribbon with no handles is drawn as one smooth curve through every point. Handles change only the segments on each side of the point that has them.

Sketch a new ribbon, close a loop

The Curve tab holds the two things you do to a ribbon as a whole.

How to

  1. Tap Sketch new ribbon. Draw one stroke across the canvas. When you let go it becomes a ribbon of eight points, in gold, and it is the one you are editing.
  2. The button reads Stop sketching while it waits for a stroke. Tap it to leave without drawing.
  3. Closed loop joins the last point of the ribbon to its first, so it becomes a ring. Untick it to open the loop again. With the group ticked, every ribbon in the group closes.
Good to know A sketched ribbon takes the plain material, a gold ink and a small shake. Change them in the Ribbon tab.
Good to know There is no delete. A ribbon you do not want can be made thin and moved off the edge, or the piece can be chosen again to start over.

The Ribbon tab

Everything a ribbon is made of, in one list. The title says which ribbon, or how many are selected.

Good to know A phone controller shows the same list, with plain sliders.

4Materials and effects

Materials

Every ribbon follows the same curve and the same width. The material is how it is painted inside that shape. Four of them move on their own.

How to

  1. Choose a material in the Ribbon tab. With the group ticked, every ribbon in it changes.
  2. Material Amount is the reach of the paint: how far Painterly warps, how dense Spray-paint and Paintbrush are, how bright the Fabric highlight is.
  3. Material Speed is how fast it moves.
Ribbon (satin)The plain flat fill, the way the pieces come
PainterlyFour soft passes of the same shape, each pushed in and out by a slow flow, so the edge breathes
Spray-paintA field of small dots across the ribbon, denser in the middle, that drifts and reseeds
PaintbrushRounded stamps laid along the ribbon, each turned a little, marching along it
FabricThe flat fill with a thin white highlight that sweeps across its width, like light on a weave
Good to know A material paints inside the ribbon’s own shape only. The rest of the canvas is never touched by it.
Good to know The painted materials use the plain blend modes. The eleven worked-out modes below apply to the satin ribbon.

Blend modes

Normal, Multiply, Darken, Lighten, Screen, Color Dodge, Color Burn, Hard Light, Soft Light, Difference, Exclusion, Hue, Saturation, Color, Luminosity, Shade, Add, Lighter Color, Darker Color, Vivid Light, Linear Light, Pin Light, Hard Mix, Linear Burn, Subtract, Divide.

Good to know The first fifteen are done by the canvas itself. The other eleven are worked out pixel by pixel for each ribbon that uses them, so a few of those on a big window cost frames.

The gap: the channel

Every ribbon first paints a band of the ground colour a little wider than itself, then paints itself inside that band. Where it crosses a ribbon beneath, the band shows as a clear channel: the gap of your own rules, kept on every crossing.

How to

  1. Move Gap in the Ribbon tab, or the Gap x Tremor pad left and right.
  2. The channel never goes under three pixels, so it reads even when the slider is at zero.
Good to know The ribbon drawn last is on top. The order is the order of the Ribbon list.

The Adjustments tab

Adjustments change the whole picture after the ribbons are drawn, on the graphics card. They are live: move a slider and the picture changes under your hand.

How to

  1. Pick a colour for Shadows, Midtones or Highlights, then raise its amt slider. At zero the colour does nothing.
  2. Tick Enable under Curves, then shape the Curve graph. Reset curve puts the graph back to a straight line.
  3. Tick Enable under Gradient Map and choose the two colours.
HueTurns every colour round the wheel, up to half a turn either way
SaturationWashes the colours out or pushes them up
BrightnessDarkens or lightens the whole picture
Shadows, Midtones, HighlightsA colour and an amount for each: the colour is pushed into the dark, the middle or the light parts
CurvesThe tone curve in the Curve graph under the tabs, once Enable is ticked
Gradient MapRepaints the picture from the Shadow color to the Highlight color by brightness
Good to know The phone has the same adjustments, as plain sliders and colour boxes, and they travel to the window at once.
Good to know If the graphics card cannot be used, the same adjustments are worked out pixel by pixel. The frame time line says so.

The Curve graph

The card under the tabs named Curve is the tone curve of the whole picture, not a ribbon. Dark is at the left and bottom, light at the right and top; the straight diagonal changes nothing.

How to

  1. Click an empty place on the graph to add a point, up to eight. Drag a point to shape the curve. The points stay in order from left to right.
  2. The curve only acts when Enable is ticked under Curves in the Adjustments tab.
  3. Reset curve in the Adjustments tab takes the points away and leaves the diagonal.
Good to know The phone has the same graph, and a point moved on either side moves on both.

The Effects tab

Seven effects on the whole picture, each a slider from nothing to full. They run on the graphics card with the adjustments.

OpacityAt full the ribbons show as painted; lower, and the whole picture fades towards the ground colour
NoiseGrain over the picture, new every frame
SharpenHardens every edge
BloomA soft light bleeding out of the bright parts
GlitchHorizontal bands torn sideways, in new places every frame
HalftoneThe picture becomes a grid of dark dots on the ground, bigger where it is darker; higher is a finer grid
Chromatic aberrationThe red and the blue of the picture pulled apart sideways
Good to know Noise and Glitch move on their own because they are rolled again every frame. Leave them at zero for a still picture.

5Sound and motion

Ambient wander

Every point of every ribbon drifts a little on its own slow wave, so the piece is never quite still. It is on when the app opens.

How to

  1. Open the Animation tab. Under Ambient wander (all ribbons), untick Enabled for a still piece.
  2. Amplitude is how far a point strays. Speed is how fast.
Good to know Wander moves the drawn ribbon, not the white dots. A dot stays where you put it; the ribbon breathes around it.

Shuffle

Tap Shuffle selected group and every ribbon in the group gets a slightly different width, taper, tremor and gap, and a new colour at random. Tap it again for another throw.

Preset A and B

Keep two states of the ribbons and glide between them.

How to

  1. Set the ribbons the way you want, then tap Save current as A. Change them, and tap Save current as B.
  2. Morph → B and Morph → A glide every ribbon’s width, taper, tremor, gap, ink and core from where it is to the saved state. The buttons wake up once a state is saved.
  3. Duration (ms) is how long the glide takes. Ease makes it start and end softly instead of at a steady pace.
Good to know A morph changes the numbers and colours, not the shape. The points stay where they are.
Good to know Choosing another piece forgets A and B.

Keyframe

One number of the group swings between two values on a clock.

How to

  1. Choose the Parameter: Width, Taper, Tremor or Gap.
  2. Set Value A and Value B, and the Duration (ms) of one pass.
  3. Interpolation: Linear is a steady pace, Ease is soft at both ends.
  4. Extend: Hold goes from A to B once and stays; Loop jumps back to A and goes again; Mirror goes A to B to A.
  5. Tap Play. The button reads Stop while it runs.
Good to know Every ribbon in the group takes the same value while the keyframe plays. Changing the piece stops it.

The two LFOs

Two slow waves, each adding a little motion to a number of the group, on top of where it already is. Each can drive a second number at the same time.

How to

  1. Open the Modulation tab. Under LFO 1 or LFO 2, tick Enabled. The value at that moment becomes the centre the wave moves around.
  2. Waveform: Sine is smooth, Square jumps between the two extremes, Triangle climbs and falls at a steady pace.
  3. Period (ms) is the length of one wave. Phase shifts where in the wave it starts, so the two LFOs can be set against each other.
  4. Amount is how far the wave pushes. Target is the number it pushes: Width, Taper, Tremor or Gap.
  5. Also drives gives the same wave a second number, with its own Amount 2.
  6. Hold (freeze) stops the wave and leaves the number at its centre.
Good to know To move a number an LFO is driving, untick Enabled, set the number, then tick it again. The LFO remembers the centre it started from, not the slider.
Good to know LFO 1 comes set for Tremor at two seconds, LFO 2 for Gap at three, both off.

Sound: the microphone

The loudness of the room pushes a number of the group. The frequency meter under the pads shows what the microphone hears.

How to

  1. Open the Modulation tab and tap Enable microphone. Windows asks once. The button reads Microphone live, and the meter starts moving.
  2. Tick Audio drives param. The value at that moment becomes the floor the sound lifts from.
  3. Audio target is the number: Width, Taper, Tremor or Gap. Audio amount is how far a loud sound pushes it.
Good to know The meter keeps drawing whether or not the sound is driving anything, so you can watch the room.
Good to know If the button reads Mic denied/unavailable, allow the microphone for the app in Windows and tap it again.
Good to know The microphone lives in the window. The phone cannot start it.

Light: the camera

The brightness of what the webcam sees pushes a number of the group. Cover the lens and it falls; shine a light and it rises.

How to

  1. Tap Enable camera in the Modulation tab. The button reads Camera live.
  2. Tick Camera drives param, choose the Camera target, set the Camera amount.
Good to know The camera picture is never shown and never leaves the app. Only its average brightness is read, from a sixteen by sixteen thumbnail.
Good to know This is not the hands page. The hands page is its own camera, in its own browser tab, and reads gestures.

Drawing like your hand

Arriving: three hand modes, Replay, Imitate and Shake, built from the strokes you draw in PL Paint, so a ribbon can move at your speed with your swell and your shake. They wait for your first drawing session in PL Paint and are not in the app yet.

6The phone controller

Connect the phone

The window serves a controller to any phone on the same Wi-Fi, or on your Tailscale. No install: it is a web page, and it can sit on the Home Screen like an app.

How to

  1. Open Third Eye on the laptop. Under the frame time in the sidebar it says Phone: and an address that ends in :8917.
  2. On the phone, open that address in the browser. The controller appears, in the same cream and gold.
  3. To keep it: on an iPhone tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, then Add. It opens full screen, upright, with no browser around it.
  4. More than one phone can connect. Each sees the same state.
Good to know The address is the laptop’s address on your network. If the laptop moves to another network the address changes; read it off the sidebar again.
Good to know The page is plain http on your own network, so the phone cannot keep it for offline use; it needs the window running.

What is on the phone

The phone is the sidebar without the canvas. The controls work the same; the sliders and colour boxes are the phone’s own.

How to

  1. At the top: the wordmark, the piece menu, the Ribbon list with All ribbons and Just this one.
  2. Then the two pads, the Curve graph, and the frequency meter, which stays blank on the phone because the microphone lives in the window.
  3. Then three folders: Ribbon params (Material, the numbers, Ink, Core, Glow, Outline color, Blend mode), Adjustments (the same sliders and colours as the window, with Curve LUT enabled, Reset curve and Gradient map), and Effects (the seven sliders).
  4. Animation and Modulation are not on the phone. Wander, the keyframe, the LFOs, sound and light are started from the window, and the phone sees their values move.
Good to know The phone has no canvas, so it cannot move a point or sketch a ribbon. Shape in the window; play from the phone.
Good to know A ticked box on a phone row is the group on the phone only; each device keeps its own selection.

How the two stay in step

Every change on either side is sent within a tenth of a second to the other, as the whole state of the piece. The last change wins.

How to

  1. Move a slider on the phone and the ribbon in the window changes. Move one in the window and the phone’s slider moves.
  2. A phone that opens, or comes back, gets the current state at once.
  3. If the window closes, the phone keeps trying every second and a half and reconnects when the window is back.
Good to know Two people moving the same slider at the same time will fight; whoever moved last is where it stays.

Your hands: the camera page

Open the controller address with /hands.html on the end, on any device with a camera. A mirrored picture of you, a status pill, and a line of numbers. Your hand drives one ribbon, with nothing to hold.

How to

  1. Allow the camera when the browser asks. The pill reads relay connected, show a hand, then tracking.
  2. The readout under the pill says which ribbon your hand holds, and its width, taper, tremor and gap as they change.
  3. Open your hand after a fist and the ribbon carries on from where it froze, not from wherever your hand is now.
Pinch, thumb to index fingerWidth: closed is thin, open is wide
Raise or lower the handTaper: high is a long taper
Roll the handTremor
Spread two hands apartGap, only while both hands are in the picture
Hold an open palm still for one secondThe next ribbon
Make a fistFreeze: the ribbon holds, and your hand can drop
Leave the pictureThe ribbon holds where it was
Good to know The hand page has its own idea of which ribbon it holds, starting at ribbon 1. The open palm moves it on; the window’s selection does not.
Good to know A resting hand sends nothing: small changes are ignored and a big jump is spread over several frames, so a tracking slip cannot throw a ribbon across its range.
Good to know The hand can push width and tremor past the top of the sliders. The slider sits at its end until the hand brings it back.
Good to know The hand tracker is fetched from the web the first time the page opens, so that device needs a connection then. Your picture never leaves the page; only the four numbers go to the window.
Good to know If the pill reads camera denied, allow the camera and reload. relay lost means the window is closed; it retries on its own.

7Recording and presenting

Presentation mode

Press F, or the round button in the top right corner, and the sidebar goes, the window fills the screen, and the dots and handles are not drawn. Only the piece remains.

How to

  1. Press F or tap the button. The window goes full screen.
  2. Play from the phone or with your hands while it shows.
  3. Press F again, or Escape, or leave full screen, and the sidebar comes back.
Good to know The button fades almost away in presentation mode and shows itself when the pointer passes over it.
Good to know The canvas is drawn at the window’s full size and the screen’s own pixel density, so a projector or a second screen gets a sharp picture.

Recording a set

Third Eye does not record video itself. It gives you the clean frame; a screen recorder, or the capture in your streaming app, takes it from there.

How to

  1. Put the window in presentation mode.
  2. Record the screen, or that window, with the recorder you use.
  3. Drive the set from the phone and your hands, so nothing of the sidebar appears.
Good to know Watch the frame time line before you go to presentation mode. A frame over a sixtieth of a second shows a warning sign; fewer worked-out blend modes, or a smaller window, brings it down.
Good to know Noise and Glitch roll again every frame, so a recording of them never repeats.

  • Width A share of the shorter side of the canvas
  • Taper How far in the ends thin
  • Tremor A shake in both edges
  • Gap The clear channel through what is beneath
  • Outline width and colour A line around the ribbon
  • Material amount and speed How much and how fast a painted material moves
  • Ink The colour
  • Core A second colour down the middle, where the piece has one
  • Glow A soft light in the ink colour, where the piece has one
  • Blend mode Twenty-six, the Photoshop names
  • Any new number gets a slider A numeric field added to a piece shows up in the tab and on the phone with no new code

Materials and effects

  • Ribbon (satin) The plain flat fill
  • Painterly Four soft passes warped by a slow flow
  • Spray-paint A drifting field of dots, denser in the middle
  • Paintbrush Turned stamps marching along the ribbon
  • Fabric A white highlight sweeping across the width
  • The channel Every ribbon cuts a ground-coloured gap through the ones beneath, never under three pixels
  • Fifteen canvas blend modes Normal to Luminosity
  • Eleven worked-out blend modes Shade, Add, Lighter Color, Darker Color, Vivid Light, Linear Light, Pin Light, Hard Mix, Linear Burn, Subtract, Divide
  • Hue, Saturation, Brightness On the whole picture
  • Colour balance A colour and an amount for shadows, midtones and highlights
  • Tone curve Up to eight points on the Curve graph, with Enable and Reset curve
  • Gradient map Shadow colour to highlight colour by brightness
  • Opacity Fades the picture towards the ground
  • Noise
  • Sharpen
  • Bloom
  • Glitch Torn bands, new every frame
  • Halftone A grid of dark dots on the ground
  • Chromatic aberration Red and blue pulled apart
  • Effects on the graphics card One pass for every adjustment and effect, with a pixel-by-pixel fallback that says so

Sound and motion

  • Ambient wander Every point drifts on its own slow wave; on when the app opens
  • Shuffle selected group A random throw of width, taper, tremor, gap and colour
  • Preset A and B Save two states, glide between them, with a duration and an ease
  • Keyframe One number swings between two values: linear or eased, hold, loop or mirror
  • LFO 1 and LFO 2 Sine, square or triangle; period, phase, amount, target
  • LFO second target Also drives, with its own amount
  • LFO hold Freezes the wave at its centre
  • Microphone Loudness pushes a number; the frequency meter shows the room
  • Camera brightness The light on the webcam pushes a number
  • Hand modes (arriving) Replay, Imitate and Shake from your PL Paint strokes; not in the app yet

The phone controller

  • Served by the window Any phone on the same Wi-Fi or Tailscale, no install
  • Add to Home Screen Full screen, upright, no browser around it
  • Several phones at once
  • Piece menu, Ribbon list, pads, Curve graph on the phone
  • Ribbon params, Adjustments, Effects folders Plain sliders, colour boxes and menus
  • Changes both ways within a tenth of a second Last change wins
  • Reconnects on its own Every second and a half, with the current state on arrival
  • Hands page A camera page at /hands.html that drives one ribbon by gesture
  • Pinch: width
  • Hand height: taper
  • Hand roll: tremor
  • Two-hand spread: gap
  • Open palm one second: next ribbon
  • Fist: freeze The ribbon holds and resumes from where it froze
  • Hand lost: hold Leaving the picture never blanks the piece
  • Smoothing and limits A resting hand sends nothing; a jump is spread over frames
  • Picture stays on the device Only four numbers go to the window

Presenting

  • Presentation mode (F) Full screen, no sidebar, no dots or handles
  • Escape or leave full screen to return
  • Recording The clean frame for a screen recorder; the app does not record itself
  • Sharp on any screen Drawn at the window size and the screen’s pixel density
